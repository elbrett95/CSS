Holding zone — 10 Haas mills × 10.5 min cycle

Upstream feeds at ~63 sec takt. Holding zone buffers up to 6 parts. Haas bank has 10 mills running in parallel, each on a 10.5 min cycle. All 10 must be loaded before the line reaches steady-state output. 1 second = 1 minute.

What to watch for. First, the fill-up: from a cold start, takes ~10 minutes (10 takts) before the first mill finishes and the line produces its first output. During this time the holding zone stays nearly empty because every incoming part goes straight to a free mill.

Next, take 1 mill down. Net inflow into the buffer is small (~0.1 parts/min) because 9 mills can almost keep up. A 15 min outage adds ~1.5 parts to the buffer.

Now take 3 mills down. Inflow into the buffer is ~0.3 parts/min — a 15 min outage fills 4–5 buffer slots. With 6 slots, you can absorb roughly a 20 min triple-outage before back-pressuring upstream. Beyond that, weld and respot have to gate down or you stack parts on the floor.

The buffer-sizing question is: what's our worst realistic Haas bank degradation, and can the holding zone hold us through it? 6 slots × 63 sec = 6.3 min of full-rate upstream output, or ~20 min of partial-degradation absorption.
